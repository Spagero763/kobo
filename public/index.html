Where the rule comes from
Celo keeps a governance-controlled list of what may pay for gas, and Kobo reads it on every health check rather than assuming. NGNm is on it. cNGN is not. That is why the fee lands where it does, and if that ever changes the app will say so instead of failing at your wallet.

Ajo, onchain. Everyone pays the same amount each round and takes a turn collecting. Members pay each other directly, so there is no pot sitting anywhere for anyone to run off with.

What happens if someone stops paying

Be clear about this before you put money in. The contract records who is in the circle and whose turn it is. It does not hold the money and it cannot make anyone pay, so it cannot recover a missed round for you. What it does give you is a public record: every contribution is a transaction, so who paid and who did not is visible to everyone, permanently, and cannot be argued about.

That is the same trust model as the ajo you already know, where the group is the enforcement. The difference is that the bookkeeping cannot be disputed and nobody is holding the pot. Join circles with people you would already lend money to.

Order is the order people joined, and it is fixed once the circle starts. Nobody can jump the queue, and no shuffle happens that you would have to trust.

For agents

Kobo is not only a page. There is a skill file and an MCP server at /mcp, so software can send naira, quote a rate or read a circle without a browser. Same rails, same contracts.

One call is paid. GET /v1/receipt/:hash tells an agent whether a payment really landed and paid who it should, for $0.01 over x402 in USD₮ or USDC. The agent signs, the Celo facilitator settles, and nobody needs gas. Prices are at /v1/paid.

The API, with real responses

Every response below was produced by calling this server when the page was built, not written by hand. Errors are always {"error": "..."} with a 4xx, phrased to be acted on rather than logged.

What the fields mean, so nothing has to be guessed:

amount, arrives
What you asked to send, and what lands with the recipient. They are equal on purpose: the fee is charged on top, never taken out of the transfer, so nobody receives less than the number you typed.
estimatedFee, maximumFee
What it should cost, and the ceiling you authorise. Under EIP-1559 you pay base plus tip and the rest is refunded, so the real cost lands near the estimate. Check affordability against the maximum, not the estimate, or a busy block turns a quote that said yes into a transfer that fails.
feeCurrency
Which token the fee comes out of, chosen from what the sender already holds. It is not always the token being sent, because cNGN cannot pay for its own gas. Never CELO.
sufficient, gasSufficient
Whether the balance covers the transfer, and separately whether anything on hand can cover the fee. They are separate because the two can come from different tokens. When either is false, note says what to do about it.
feeOptions
Every token Celo accepts as gas that this wallet holds, with the fee priced in each. Shown so a caller can see why one was picked rather than trusting the choice.
payouts
On /v1/currencies: the currencies a naira balance can be paid out in, not currencies you can hold here. Send naira, the recipient gets shillings.
Fees are paid in naira or dollars, never CELO, in wallets that support it.
